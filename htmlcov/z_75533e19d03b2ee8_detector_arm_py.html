23@dataclass(frozen=True, kw_only=True) 

24class DetectorArm: 

25 """One detector a campaign competes, under a name of its own. 

26 

27 What separates one of these from another is what a window is read as — an encoder's 

28 states, pretrained or at their initialisation, the cells the encoder would read, the 

29 summaries per channel or their counts of readings, the error of what is filled in under a 

30 backbone's masks, or each channel against its own history — and the knobs of the detector 

31 itself: which distance from the fitted windows it scores by and the neighbours that distance 

32 is taken to, or which cells are hidden, the draws an error is averaged over and how the 

33 errors are read, or how far back a channel's history reaches and how its departures make 

34 the score; the share of the fitting side the alarm flags and what a score is read against. 

35 A variant of an arm is the arm with one of those turned, or the encoder's layer or pooling 

36 turned. 

37 

38 Invariants: an arm over an encoder's states names the model whose shape it reads; an arm 

39 that asks the pretext names the backbone whose masks it asks under and no shape; one over 

40 the summaries or its channels' history names neither. 

41 

42 Attributes: 

43 ref: What the campaign calls this detector. 

44 features: What a window is read as. 

45 architecture: Artifact of the model whose shape an encoder has; ``None`` for the 

46 summaries. 

47 backbone: Artifact of the pretrained weights; ``None`` for the shape at its 

48 initialisation, and for the summaries. 

49 encoder: Which readings the encoder is given and which layer it is read at. 

50 pooling: How the states of a window become the one state the detector reads. 

51 distance: Which distance from the fitted windows a detector over features scores by. 

52 neighbours: How many nearest fitted windows that distance is the mean to, where it is 

53 the distance to the nearest. 

54 contamination: The share of fitting windows the alarm is set to flag. 

55 reference: What a window's score is read against before the alarm. 

56 draws: How many draws of the backbone's masks an error is averaged over. 

57 hidden: Which cells the pretext is asked to fill in. 

58 normalisation: How the cells' errors become a window's score. 

59 events: How many of the fitting side's events a detector over features is taught. 

60 head: How a taught detector scores a window by its events. 

61 history: How far back a detector over its channels' history reads, what departs and how 

62 the channels' departures make the score. 

63 """
