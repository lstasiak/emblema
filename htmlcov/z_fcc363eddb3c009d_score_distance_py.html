4class ScoreDistance(StrEnum): 

5 """How far a window's features lie from the normal ones: the distance a detector scores by. 

6 

7 Every feature is standardised over the fitting side either way. A distance to the nearest 

8 fitting windows then weighs the leading directions of the fitting variation most, since the 

9 features are correlated and those directions carry most of their spread; a change spread 

10 thinly over many directions barely moves it. A whitened distance weighs every direction of 

11 the fitting variation alike, so it reads such a change at its full size, and it reads a 

12 month that drifted along a leading direction as less unusual than the nearest-neighbour 

13 distance does. 

14 

15 Three more are the bars the field holds a representation to: the plain squared distance from 

16 the fitting mean, which weighs every feature's departure alike; the farthest single feature's 

17 departure, which flags a window for one reading far out, as a threshold on a channel's 

18 deviations would; and the classical reading of principal components, how far a window lies 

19 within the subspace the leading components span and how far outside it. 

20 

21 Attributes: 

22 NEAREST_NEIGHBOURS: The mean Euclidean distance to the nearest fitting windows. 

23 WHITENED: The squared Mahalanobis distance from the fitting mean under the fitting 

24 side's covariance, shrunk so it inverts whatever the side's size. 

25 CENTRE: The squared Euclidean distance from the fitting mean, features standardised. 

26 FARTHEST: The largest standardised departure of any one feature from the fitting mean. 

27 SUBSPACE: Hotelling's statistic within the leading components' subspace plus the squared 

28 residual outside it, each over its fitting median. 

29 """ 

30 

31 NEAREST_NEIGHBOURS = "neighbours" 

32 WHITENED = "whitened" 

33 CENTRE = "centre" 

34 FARTHEST = "farthest" 

35 SUBSPACE = "subspace"
